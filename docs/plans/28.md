# Plan — #28 Spike: confirm the client actually renders camera roll

## What the ticket asks

Answer one question: **does the client render camera roll?** CLAUDE.md verifies roll is
*representable* in the protocol. Nothing has confirmed it is *honoured*. Decision 4 and all of
Phase 0 rest on it.

## Honest scope limit — read this first

**This change cannot answer the question. It can only ask it.**

The answer requires `runServer`, a connected client, and a human looking at a horizon. None of
that is verifiable from a tool call or a unit test. So the deliverable here is the
**instrument**: a throwaway command that puts a known roll on the camera. The verdict is a
human observation, recorded as a comment on #28, and the ticket stays open until then.

That is the correct shape for a spike, but it means this PR merging is *not* progress on the
question — flying it is.

## Approach

Two subcommands under the existing `/fpv`:

- **`/fpv rolltest [degrees] [offset]`** — attaches the camera to the local player, first person,
  and applies roll. Defaults to 30°.

  The angle is an argument deliberately: a single fixed value cannot distinguish "roll works"
  from "roll is clamped" or "roll snaps to increments". Trying 0/15/30/90/180 answers that in one
  session.

  **Two rotation modes, because the ticket asks which `rotationType` is required.**
  `Custom` (default) sets an *absolute* rotation, which freezes yaw and pitch — it answers "can
  the client draw a rolled camera at all". But that is not the shape decision 4 needs: a drone
  camera tracks the aircraft's yaw and pitch and rolls *on top of them*. `offset=true` uses
  `AttachedToPlusOffset` with `rotationOffset` to test that composition. Roll could plausibly
  work in one mode and not the other, and only the second one generalises to #19 — so a "yes"
  from `Custom` alone would be a misleading pass.

  `isLocked` follows the mode: locked for absolute (that is what makes a fixed rotation
  observable), unlocked for offset (the point there is roll riding on a live look direction).
  `isLocked`'s exact effect is itself an unknown to record.
- **`/fpv rollreset`** — sends the reset packet. Without this a pilot who tries 90° is stuck
  looking sideways with no way back, which would be a genuinely hostile thing to ship even in a
  throwaway.

Both extend `AbstractTargetPlayerCommand` for the free self-defaulting `PlayerRef`.

`FpvCommand` gains two `addSubCommand` calls and keeps its current config-report behaviour on
bare `/fpv`.

## Test strategy

**No unit tests, deliberately.** Per CLAUDE.md, `:fpv-plugin` has no tests — adapters are
verified by flying — and `:fpv-core` is where the tested logic lives. This change is pure
adapter: it constructs a packet and writes it. A test asserting
`settings.rotation.roll == radians(30)` would restate the implementation and pass whether or not
the client honours roll, which is the only thing anyone cares about here.

Nothing lands in `:fpv-core`, so decision 10 is not touched. Verification is `./gradlew build`
plus the flight test below.

## The flight test (the actual deliverable)

1. `./gradlew runServer`, connect a client.
2. `/fpv rolltest` — expect a horizon tilted 30°, view frozen.
3. `/fpv rollreset` — expect the normal view back.
4. `/fpv rolltest 30 true` — expect a 30° tilt that **stays tilted while you look around**. This
   is the one that matters for #19.
5. Sweep both modes: `0`, `15`, `30`, `90`, `180`. Continuous, or snapped/clamped?
6. Record on #28: does it tilt in each mode; is it continuous; which `rotationType` worked; what
   `isLocked` actually did; and is `Direction.pitch`'s sign what `ControlInput`'s javadoc warns it
   might not be.

A result of "works in `Custom`, ignored in `AttachedToPlusOffset`" is not a pass. It would mean
roll cannot compose with a tracked entity, which is what decision 4 requires — flag it loudly
rather than recording a yes.

## Risks

- **The client may ignore roll entirely.** That is the finding, not a bug to fix. It reopens
  decision 4 and reshapes Phase 0 — which is exactly why this runs before #13–#15.
- **`skipCharacterPhysics` is not exercised here.** The spike attaches to the local player, not
  a drone entity, so it does not prove roll survives the real decision-4 setup. #19 must
  re-confirm against an actual attached entity. Noted so it is not mistaken for settled.
- **Throwaway code.** Both commands are scaffolding for one question. If the answer is yes, #19
  supersedes them and they should be deleted, not extended.
