# Plan — #13 Quad physics integrator with a rate-agnostic `step(dt)`

Ticket: [#13](https://github.com/maartenpeels/hytale_fpv_drone/issues/13) · Epic: #4 · Milestone: v0.1 — Feel

## What the ticket asks for

A quad flight model in `:fpv-core`: `(state, input, dt) -> state`. Thrust from four motors,
gravity, linear and angular drag, momentum. `step(dt)` must be rate-agnostic — changing
`World.setTps` may change how smoothly the drone is sampled, never how it flies (CLAUDE.md
decision 3).

Done when tests cover hover, ballistic fall, terminal velocity, and dt-invariance.

## Scope boundary against #14 and #15

#13 is the *dynamics*. The two things sitting upstream of it have their own tickets, so this
change installs the seams and a deliberately dumb placeholder at each:

```
ControlInput (stick positions)
   │
   ├─ linear stick→rate map  ......... placeholder; #15 replaces with rate/expo curves
   ▼  BodyRates demand (rad/s)
   ├─ proportional rate tracker ...... placeholder; #14 replaces with the PID
   ▼  normalised torque demand (−1..1 per axis)
   ├─ MotorMixer ..................... #13, final
   ▼  MotorOutputs (4 × 0..1)
   └─ forces, torques, integration ... #13, final
```

Both placeholders are one method each, documented as such. Neither gets a premature interface —
#14 and #15 will introduce those when they have a second implementation to justify them.

## Verified facts this design rests on

Read out of `~/.gradle/caches/hytale-decompiled/release-0.5.9/server/com/hypixel/hytale/`, not
assumed. These are new; they are not yet in CLAUDE.md's "Verified facts" section.

| Fact | Source |
| --- | --- |
| `PhysicsConstants.GRAVITY_ACCELERATION = 32.0` | `server/core/modules/physics/util/PhysicsConstants.java:4` |
| Gravity acts on `force.y` and is negated → **+Y is up** | `server/core/modules/physics/util/ForceProviderStandard.java:36,45` |
| `protocol.Position` is `double x, y, z` | `protocol/Position.java:17-19` |
| `setYawPitch`: `x = -sin(yaw)cos(pitch)`, `y = sin(pitch)`, `z = -cos(yaw)cos(pitch)` | `math/vector/Vector3dUtil.java:57-63` |

Three consequences:

1. **Gravity defaults to 32.0, not 9.81.** Hytale's world is not metric-consistent. A drone
   built around 9.81 would feel like it was flying on the moon next to everything else in the
   world.
2. **State is `double`.** The protocol is already `double`, and 240 integrations/second
   accumulates visible float drift in position.
3. **Hytale's `Direction.pitch` is positive nose-*up*** (`y = sin(pitch)`), which is the
   opposite of `ControlInput.pitch`. CLAUDE.md told the reader to verify this rather than assume;
   now verified — they disagree, and the #19 adapter must negate. Hytale's yaw also increases
   toward `−X`, i.e. opposite to a right-handed yaw about `+Y`.

## Frame and sign conventions

Fixed here once, tested, and documented on the types. Everything downstream inherits them.

- **World**: right-handed, `+Y` up, gravity `(0, −g, 0)`. Matches Hytale.
- **Body axes at identity orientation coincide with world axes**: `+X` right, `+Y` up (the
  thrust axis), `−Z` forward. This is the OpenGL view convention, and `−Z` forward matches
  Hytale's yaw-0 heading, which keeps the #19 adapter to a yaw sign flip rather than a
  180° offset.
- **Rates are carried as `BodyRates(roll, pitch, yaw)` in `ControlInput`'s sign convention** —
  roll+ banks right, pitch+ is nose *down*, yaw+ is nose right — not as a raw axis vector. In
  the frame above all three happen to be *negative*-axis rotations (`roll` about `−Z`, `pitch`
  about `−X`, `yaw` about `−Y`), because the pilot convention is transmitter-facing while the
  frame is view-facing. That conversion lives in exactly one tested method,
  `BodyRates.toBodyAxes()`, so nobody has to hold three sign flips in their head.
- Units throughout: Hytale world units (blocks), seconds, radians.

## Motor model

Four motors on an X frame, named rather than indexed (`frontLeft`, `frontRight`, `rearLeft`,
`rearRight`) because motor-index conventions differ between flight-controller firmwares and an
off-by-one here is invisible until you fly it.

Mixing happens in **command** space, linearly:

```
FL = collective + roll + pitch + yaw
FR = collective − roll + pitch − yaw
RL = collective + roll − pitch − yaw
RR = collective − roll − pitch + yaw
```

Yaw signs assume `FL`/`RR` spin CCW and `FR`/`RL` spin CW viewed from above; frame reaction
torque is opposite to prop spin, so increasing the CCW pair yaws the airframe right.

Desaturation, then thrust:

- If `max − min > 1` the three torque terms are scaled by `1/(max − min)` and remixed —
  collective is never sacrificed to attitude authority first.
- The result is then shifted to fit `[0, 1]` and clamped.
- **Per-motor thrust is `command²`**, not `command`. This is the physically right shape and it
  matters for feel: it puts hover at `sqrt(1/TWR) ≈ 35 %` stick, where real quads hover,
  instead of `1/TWR ≈ 12 %`.

Because thrust is quadratic while mixing is linear, achieved torque does not equal demanded
torque. That is correct — it is precisely the error a real rate PID exists to close, and #14
inherits it rather than a model that pretends the problem away.

Forces and torques come out of the *achieved* squared thrusts `t_i`:

```
thrustAccel  = maxThrustAccel · (t_FL + t_FR + t_RL + t_RR) / 4      along body up
rollDiff     = ((t_FL + t_RL) − (t_FR + t_RR)) / 2
pitchDiff    = ((t_FL + t_FR) − (t_RL + t_RR)) / 2
yawDiff      = ((t_FL + t_RR) − (t_FR + t_RL)) / 2
```

Each `*Diff ∈ [−1, 1]` is scaled by a per-axis authority (rad/s²), yaw's being much weaker than
roll/pitch as on a real quad. Attitude authority therefore falls away at idle throttle and
saturates at full throttle, for free, without a special case.

## Integration

Semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler on velocity then position; quaternion integrated as
`q' = normalise(q + ½ · q ⊗ ω · dt)`. Standard, stable under drag, and cheap enough to run 240×
a second for 8 pilots.

Drag is `−(k₁·v + k₂·|v|·v)` — both a linear and a quadratic coefficient. Quadratic is the
physically honest one and gives a realistic terminal velocity and the "wall of air" a pilot
feels at speed; the linear term is kept for gentle low-speed bleed-off. Angular drag is linear
in body rate.

Consistent first-order integration is what makes the dt-invariance test meaningful: error is
`O(dt)`, so halving `dt` halves the discrepancy, and the test asserts a bounded relative
difference rather than exact equality.

## Test strategy

Following `ControlInputTest`'s conventions — `@Nested` per behaviour group, names that state the
*reason*.

The four the ticket names:

- `hoversAtSteadyThrottleBecauseThrustCancelsGravity` — collective at `sqrt(1/TWR)`, level, no
  drag; velocity stays at zero over hundreds of steps.
- `fallsBallisticallyAtZeroThrottle` — drag zeroed, closed form `−½gt²` to tight tolerance.
- `convergesOnTerminalVelocityFromQuadraticDrag` — asserts `sqrt(g/k₂)`, and that it is
  approached from below and not overshot.
- `landsInTheSamePlaceWhenDtIsHalvedAndStepsDoubled` — the one that protects decision 3's escape
  hatch. Run at 30 TPS × 8 substeps versus × 16, and again at 240 TPS, with a non-trivial
  constant input so attitude, thrust direction and drag all move. Bounded relative difference on
  both position *and* attitude.

Plus, because these are the things impossible to check by flying:

- `Vec3`/`Quat` algebra, and that quaternion integration stays normalised over 10⁵ steps.
- `BodyRates.toBodyAxes` sign convention, asserted against physical statements: a positive roll
  rate moves the body right axis downward, positive pitch moves the nose down, positive yaw
  moves the nose toward the body right axis.
- Mixer: neutral demand leaves four equal commands; each axis in isolation drives the correct
  pair up and pair down; a demand that would exceed `[0,1]` scales torque rather than clipping
  one motor; commands never leave `[0,1]`.
- Throttle-dependent authority: identical roll demand at idle and mid throttle yields less
  angular acceleration at idle.
- Determinism: the same `(state, input, dt)` twice gives bit-identical output.
- Non-finite and out-of-range parameters are rejected at construction.

`:fpv-core` currently contains only `ControlInput`, which is fully tested. There is no existing

## Measured results

Numbers from the finished model, so the tolerances in the tests are justified rather than
guessed. Input is a gentle off-axis cruise; distances are world units.

| Measurement | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 240 vs 480 substeps over 1 s | travelled 15.65, disagreed by **0.058 (0.37 %)** |
| Convergence ratio when `dt` halves | **0.492** — textbook first-order `O(dt)` |
| 30 TPS × 8 vs 240 TPS × 8 over 2 s | travelled 58.51, disagreed by **0.247 (0.42 %)** |
| Attitude, 240 vs 480 substeps | nose direction differs by **0.0013** |
| 10 s of hover | altitude drift **4 × 10⁻⁵**, residual speed **7 × 10⁻⁶** |
| 1 s ballistic fall vs continuous solution | −16.067 vs −16.0, exactly half a step of velocity |

The 0.42 % figure is the one that matters for decision 3: taking the escape hatch and running a
drone world at 240 TPS moves the drone about 25 cm differently over 58 m of flight. That is
comfortably inside gate-detection noise, so raising the tick rate stays a config change and does
not become a re-tune.

## Risks and open questions

1. **Drag is linear + quadratic with hand-picked defaults.** Defaults are `k₂ = 0.01` (free-fall
   terminal ≈ 57 u/s) and `k₁ = 0.1`. These are guesses about *feel*, not measurements — #24 is
   where they get judged, and they are parameters precisely so that judging them is a config
   change.
2. **Thrust-to-weight defaults to 8.0**, mid-range for a racing quad. Same story.
3. **No Euler-angle readout on `Quat`.** #19 needs orientation as Hytale's
   `Direction{yaw,pitch,roll}`; adding the conversion now, with no consumer and no test that can
   assert the *Hytale* half of the mapping, would be speculative. Deferred to #19, where the
   packet is in hand. Flagging it so it is not a surprise.
4. **The placeholder rate tracker will feel stiff**, since a proportional tracker with no
   integral term cannot hold a rate against sustained drag. That is #14's job. If someone flies
   the #13 model standalone and reports it feels wrong, that is expected, not a regression.
5. **Semi-implicit Euler, not RK4.** Chosen for stability under drag and cheapness. If the
   dt-invariance test cannot be made to hold at a tolerance worth having, that is the signal to
   revisit — and it would be a finding worth putting in front of decision 3.
